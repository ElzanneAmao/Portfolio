The work

What this is about

Most businesses didn't do a formal AI rollout. Everyone started using it on their own terms, and the wild west period has produced a lot of content that sounds exactly like everyone else's. You and your competitor and the shop down the street now all have a product that has "levelled up" and "isn't just offering a solution — it's promising a new start." Your brand worked too hard to get lost in that noise.

With this audit I look at your content operation across channels, compare it against your brand standards and your competitors, and write up exactly where the problems are and what to prioritise.

The process

How it works

01 Step 01

Intake and access

A short onboarding form (~30 min) walks you through what to provide: content samples across channels, team structure, tools in use, and any existing brand documentation.

02 Step 02

Discovery call

A 60-minute conversation about your business goals, your audiences, how AI ended up in the workflow, and what you think is working and what isn't.

03 Step 03

Content and competitor review

I review content across channels against your brand standards, map where AI is involved in the workflow, and run a competitor positioning review including at least one alternative competitor you may not have considered.

04 Step 04

Findings and recommendations

A written report organised by priority, leading with the most pressing finding. Each finding includes what was observed, why it matters, and a recommended change.

05 Step 05

Handover call

A 60-to-90-minute session to walk through the report and discuss what comes next.

The deliverable

What you receive

A written report of about ten pages, specific to your business. It leads with the most pressing finding (not a generic overview), includes a competitor positioning review with messaging angles you can actually use, and is clear about what you can fix yourself and what would need outside help. Written so you can act on it or hand it to your leadership team and have it make sense to them too.

Who this is for

  • Founders and owner-operators who can tell the content has changed since AI entered the picture but haven't had the time to figure out where it went wrong
  • Marketing leads at small-to-medium businesses who need an outside perspective on what's actually happening across channels
  • Leadership teams about to make a decision on AI tooling and want an honest read on the current state first

The audit is a standalone service that you can implement yourself. I can also help build your content operations so your team can work more effectively with AI.

This methodology was developed through extensive work in multichannel content operations, education marketing, and multi-brand content strategy. Pricing is scoped per engagement and discussed after an initial conversation.

Frequently asked questions

What does the audit assess?

The audit covers four areas. First, a content inventory and channel mapping: what you're producing, where, how frequently, and who's involved. Second, a quality and consistency assessment across your active channels, checking both structural elements (value propositions, calls to action, audience specificity) and voice and authenticity (does this sound like your brand, or could it belong to anyone). Third, a competitor content analysis covering three to five direct competitors and their positioning. Fourth, a gap analysis identifying where your content operation is falling short, prioritised by business impact.

What do I get at the end?

A written report of approximately ten pages, plus a 60-to-90-minute handover call to walk through the findings. The report includes an executive summary, a picture of your current content operation, findings organised by priority (each with a specific observation, why it matters, and a recommended change), a competitor landscape section, and a prioritised list of next steps. Recommendations are directional: they tell you what to change and why, with enough specificity to act on.

How long does it take?

About two weeks from the discovery call to the handover call. The discovery call is an hour. The content review, competitor analysis, and report writing take three to eight days depending on the scope of your content operation. The handover call follows within a few days of the report being ready.

How much does it cost?

The audit is a project fee based on the scope of your content operation: primarily the number of channels, the volume of content, and how many people are involved in producing it. I can give you a specific number after a short conversation about what you're working with.

What do I need to provide before we start?

Content samples across your active channels (as much history as you have; a year is ideal), a summary of who on your team produces, approves, and publishes content, a list of the AI tools in use and how they're being used, and any existing brand documentation (style guides, tone of voice documents, content briefs). If you don't have documentation, that's noted as a finding in itself.

Is this just a ChatGPT-generated report?

No. The audit involves a working session with you, a manual review of your content across channels, and a competitor analysis. AI tools assist with parts of the research, but the quality and voice assessment (the core of the audit) is a human judgment call based on 16 years of content strategy and brand communication experience. The report is written for your specific business, not generated from a template.

What if the audit finds that our content is fine?

Then the report says that, with specifics about what's working and why. The audit is a diagnostic: it reports what it finds, not what justifies further work. If your content is strong and your AI workflow is producing consistent, on-brand output, that's a useful finding too. It means you can invest your time and budget elsewhere.

What happens after the audit?

The audit is a complete, standalone deliverable. You can act on the recommendations yourself, brief an agency, or bring in another consultant. If the findings point to a need for a structured content system, we can discuss the system design engagement, but there's no obligation to continue working with me, and the audit report gives you everything you need to move forward independently.

Can't I just audit our own content internally?

You can review your content, but the value of an external audit is the perspective. People inside a business develop familiarity with their own content that makes it difficult to notice gradual drift. You read your own website every day, so you stop seeing where the tone has shifted or where the messaging has become generic. An external assessment catches what internal familiarity obscures, and the competitor analysis provides context you don't have from inside the operation.
