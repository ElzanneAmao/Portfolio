The work

What this is about

AI can improve your content operation, but getting from "everyone is using it however they see fit" to "we have a system" is hard to do from inside the team while you're also running it. I come in, map how your team works, design the system around the tools you already use, build the prompt systems for each channel, and train the team to run it on their own.

The process

A five-phase build

01 Phase 01

Brand and content audit

This phase uses findings from a content audit: either one I've done, or one from a previous agency within the last three months. If you haven't had one, we start there.

02 Phase 02

Operational audit

Mapping the current tools, who creates what content, how approvals work, and where the bottlenecks are. I'm also looking for who owns the prompt system.

03 Phase 03

System design

This is the blueprint, visually documented for your team, to use as their source of truth.

04 Phase 04

Custom AI content system

Building channel-specific prompt systems calibrated to your brand voice, using whatever AI tool your team already works with. I'll provide custom prompts designed around how your brand sounds.

05 Phase 05

Handover and training

Team training run from the system documentation itself, so the material and the method are consistent. Separate session for the prompt skill owner on maintenance and iteration.

The deliverable

What you receive

A working content system built for your team and documented so it can outlast the engagement. Specifically:

  • End-to-end documented workflow from brief to publication
  • Prompt skill documents for each content channel
  • Tool integration map showing how AI fits the existing stack
  • Quick reference guide for daily use
  • Change log template so the system can evolve without starting over

Who this is for

Small and medium businesses who have done the diagnostic work (whether through an audit with me or separately) and are ready to build. Ideally you have a clear picture of where the content operation is breaking down and a team that will own the system once it's built.

All work is done collaboratively with your team, not handed over as a finished artefact. The system only works if the people running it were part of building it.

Frequently asked questions

What does the system include?

The deliverable is a documented content operations system with several components: an end-to-end workflow from brief to publication, a RACI matrix defining who decides what at each stage, one prompt skill document per channel (written in markdown, platform-agnostic), a tool integration map, a change log, a quick reference guide, and a team training session. The prompt skill documents encode your brand voice, messaging standards, and quality expectations into structured prompts your team uses in their AI tools.

Do I need to have done an audit first?

The system design requires an understanding of your brand, its voice, and where the content is currently falling short. That can come from my audit (the natural entry point) or from equivalent work done independently, but the input needs to exist. I also run an operational audit as part of the system design engagement itself, mapping how your team currently works, which tools they use, and where the workflow breaks down. The system is designed against real findings, not assumptions.

What happens after handover?

You run it. The system is designed so your team operates it independently. Someone on your team is identified during the engagement as the skill owner: the person with enough brand judgment to maintain and update the prompt system over time. They receive specific training on how to maintain and adjust the system as your business evolves. There is no ongoing dependency on me after handover.

What if we change AI tools after you've built the system?

The prompt skill documents are written in markdown, which is platform-agnostic. They work in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot, Gemini, or any other tool that accepts structured text prompts. If you switch tools, the system moves with you. The workflow documentation and governance structure are also tool-independent: they describe how your team works, not which software they use.

How long does it take?

Four to five weeks for a contained operation (one brand, one to two content channels, a small team). Six to eight weeks for a more complex one (multiple brands or audience segments, three or more channels, cross-functional workflows). The timeline includes the operational audit, system design, prompt system build, documentation, and training.

How much does it cost?

The system design is a project fee based on the complexity of the build: primarily the number of channels (each requires its own prompt skill document), the team size, the number of tools involved, and whether content workflow crosses into other functions like sales or product. I'll put together a specific scope and fee after an initial conversation about what you're working with.

What's the difference between this and just writing better prompts?

A prompt is a single instruction for a single output. A content system is the infrastructure that governs how your team produces content consistently: who does what, in what order, with what tools, against what standards. The prompt skill documents are one component of the system. They encode your brand voice and channel-specific requirements into structured prompts. But they sit inside a workflow with decision rights, review stages, and a governance structure that ensures consistency across people and over time. Writing a good prompt solves one task. The system solves the organisational problem.

Will this slow my team down?

The system is designed to fit into how your team already works, not to add bureaucratic layers on top of it. The prompt skill documents replace the improvised prompting your team does now. They don't add a step, they replace an inconsistent one with a consistent one. The workflow formalises decisions that are already being made informally (who reviews, who approves, what qualifies as ready to publish). For most teams, the initial adjustment period is a few days before the new process becomes routine.

Do you provide ongoing support after the system is built?

The system design engagement ends at handover. If you want ongoing strategic support (someone to check in on how the system is working, adjust it as your business or tools evolve, and advise on content and AI decisions), that's the advisory retainer, a separate engagement with a three-month minimum. The system is designed to work without ongoing external involvement, but the retainer is available for businesses that want it.
