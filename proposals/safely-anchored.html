The main finding

The journey a family takes, as described to me.

  • A family enquires by email, WhatsApp or in person
  • Almost all of them come for a trial day
  • Almost all of those children enrol

The exceptions are children the school knows it cannot support, and families who are later offered a place at the local public school.

The problem to solve is getting more families into that journey.

“Ek dink die grootste ad is ons busse wat orals rondry — hulle ken ons busse, maar nie ons skool nie.”

The Safely Anchored name is associated with transport and aftercare. Thirty‑five parents have reviewed the transport service on Google and none have reviewed the school, which is largely the same community answering two different questions.

That is a better position than being unknown. Every bus is already a daily impression across Sunningdale, Parklands and Table View, and it carries no indication that there is a school attached to it.

An eye on the next set of enrolments

The next fifty families will need more proof.

A family who arrives on another family's recommendation:

  • Has already had the most important question answered by someone they trust, which is whether their child would be safe and cared for here
  • May still look at the website and the social accounts, usually to confirm what they were told rather than to decide
  • Reads an out‑of‑date form or a slow page as a small school being busy, and carries on

A family who has never heard of the school:

  • Has nobody's word to go on, so needs the reassurance to come from somewhere else
  • Has only the website, the Google listing, the social accounts and the enquiry and application forms as evidence
  • Uses those things to judge whether this is a school that pays attention, because there is nothing else available to judge on

Getting the word out

Three ways a family hears about the school.

Posting on Facebook and Instagram.

  • This is organic social media, and it reaches people who already follow the page
  • The school has 463 followers on Instagram and 619 on Facebook
  • Posting more on organic social media will not lead to a significant increase in enquiries or enrolments
  • How the school presents its brand on these platforms is a trust point, and a cohesive look and feel matters

Paying Meta to show a post to a wider audience.

  • Boosting posts has been done in the past but stopped since March 2026
  • Advertising through Meta can be done in a more strategic way to reach parents in Sunningdale, Parklands and Table View who do not follow the page and have not heard of the school
  • The school chooses the area, the type of parent, how much it spends and for how long, and can stop at any point

Reaching families away from a screen. Advertising is not only online, and for a school of this size the offline routes are often the stronger ones:

  • The buses, which are seen across three suburbs every weekday
  • Promoting the school to preschools nearby that do not compete for the same age group
  • Occupational therapists, speech therapists and paediatric practices, who are asked which school would suit a child
  • Opening the holiday club to children from any school
  • Churches in the area, where families are already inclined towards a school of this kind
  • Estate agents, who are asked about schools by every family moving in
